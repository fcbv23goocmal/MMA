Is a macaw right for you?

A macaw is a long relationship with a loud, clever, chewing animal. Think it through honestly. We would rather you wait than rehome a bird later.

  • They live for decadesLarge macaws commonly live for many decades in human care. Plan for the whole span, including who takes them if your life changes.
  • They are loudContact calls and screams are normal, especially at dawn and dusk. Neighbors, walls and lease terms matter.
  • They need room and companyA large cage, daily time out of it, and real attention. A bored macaw can become destructive or unhappy.
  • They need an avian vetFind one before you bring a bird home. Food, toys and vet care are ongoing costs.

Unhurried by design. You should be able to ask for anything below and get a straight answer.

  1. Start a conversationCall or text Megan. Tell her about your home, your schedule and any birds you’ve kept before.

Questions people ask

Short answers. Ask Megan for specifics on any bird.

Can I visit before I decide?

Yes. Meeting the bird in person is the best way to know if it’s a good match. Get in touch to set up a time.

Do macaws really talk?

Many learn words and phrases, but no breeder can promise it. Personality and the time you spend with a bird matter more than species.

What do macaws eat?

A varied diet of quality pellets, fresh vegetables and fruit, and nuts in moderation. Your avian vet can help tailor it.

Are macaws good with children?

They can bond with families, but their beaks are powerful and they need calm, supervised handling. Young children should never be left alone with one.

What should I buy before the bird comes home?

A large, sturdy cage, safe perches and chewable toys, a play stand, and a scale. Book your first avian vet visit ahead of time.

Are there legal rules for owning a macaw?

Rules differ by species and by state. Several macaw species are protected under international and U.S. wildlife laws, and some states and cities add their own rules. Ask Megan about the species you are considering, and check your state and local requirements before you adopt.
